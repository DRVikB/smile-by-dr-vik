# SmileCompose iOS App Store and privacy readiness

> **Superseded (2026-09-27).** This earlier review predates the native iOS app, accounts and the V1 privacy controls. The current position is in [V1_READINESS.md](V1_READINESS.md) and [docs/](docs/). It is kept for history.

**Status: engineering review, not a legal opinion or a claim of compliance.** This records the current repository behavior and the decisions required before patient use or App Store submission. Apple or a regulator makes its own assessment.

## Current architecture and data flow

- The product is a Next.js/React web app and PWA. There is no native iOS/Xcode target yet.
- The active case, patient label, design selections, generated result and recoverable session are stored in the browser's IndexedDB (`smile-temporary-case`). The case log and both image versions are in `smile-case-log`; the clinician's reference library is in `smile-case-library`; validation scores are in `smile-validation`. Small display preferences are in `localStorage`.
- **Update 2026-09-28:** signed in, the Case Library (the clinician's finished-case photos used as style references) and the optional profile photo are stored in the clinician's SmileCompose account (private Supabase Storage). App Store privacy details must now declare **Photos or Videos — collected, linked to the user, App Functionality (not tracking)**. The patient case log stays on the device.
- These stores are local to the app's browser origin; no app feature uploads the case log. (Builds without accounts keep the reference library on the device.) They are not app-level encrypted, have no automatic expiry, and browser/site-data deletion removes them. The UI supports deleting individual or all cases in each relevant library. There is no single verified “erase every local record” control.
- A live generation sends the selected photo and design inputs from the device to the SmileCompose Cloudflare Worker, which forwards them to the selected image provider. The Worker does not intentionally save photo/request bodies. Cloudflare Durable Object duplicate protection stores a random request claim and timestamp for up to 24 hours; it contains no image or patient name. HTTP responses are marked `no-store`.
- Gemini is the current live provider. Google says paid Gemini API prompts and responses are not used to improve its products, but prompts and responses may be logged for abuse monitoring for a limited period. That is off-device processing and limited provider retention; the product must not be described as zero-cloud or zero-retention while this route is enabled. The integration does not use Gemini File API, grounding, or explicit context caching.
- A paid OpenAI adapter and a generic HTTP adapter also exist in source. They are not the selected production provider, but a release should remove unused adapters/configuration or document and govern every provider that could be enabled.
- No product analytics SDK or app-level telemetry was found in the reviewed dependencies and source. Hosting/provider operational records are subject to those providers' terms and settings and should be checked separately.
- The generation flow now displays a case-specific disclosure before the first live AI request for a photo/reference pair. A clinician must attest that the patient has been informed and the practice has permission and a lawful basis. The app saves the attestation time and SHA-256 input fingerprint locally with the case and each generated result; it does not send this receipt to Google. Replacing the patient photo or selected reference requires a new confirmation. The Cloudflare handler rejects a live-provider request without the expected consent header before calling the provider. This is a clinician attestation, not a patient signature or a substitute for the practice's legal basis and notice.
- The versioned consent header is an application-flow guard against accidental unconfirmed generation, not a cryptographic proof of patient consent; the image request reaches Cloudflare before the handler can check it. Strong server-verifiable permission evidence would require an authenticated clinician/session design, which the current app does not have.

## Privacy architecture to target

1. Keep case records, images, references, preferences and review notes in an on-device database. Never upload or sync the case library. Do not add analytics, ad tracking, crash attachments or photo-bearing diagnostics.
2. For a native release, use iOS protected files or an encrypted database, device file protection, and exclude clinical stores from iCloud/device backup if the requirement is truly device-only. Add an optional Face ID app lock. Provide an understandable “erase all local cases and images” action and retention guidance. Device-only storage still needs a lost-device, shared-device and deletion policy.
3. Keep generation as a separate, explicit action. Before transmission, disclose the actual recipient(s), what is sent, purpose, current retention/processing terms and that refusal means no AI preview. Record the clinician's attestation that the patient has been informed and the practice has established the required lawful basis. Do not log the patient's name/photo as proof. The practice must choose and document its lawful basis and Article 9 condition; an in-app checkbox alone does not make processing lawful.
4. If the desired promise is that patient images never leave the device, disable cloud image generation. A server-side API key cannot be kept secret in a distributed iOS app. A genuinely local image-generation model would need to be selected, licensed, packaged, tested for device performance and validated for the intended use; there is no drop-in equivalence to Gemini in this codebase today.
5. If external AI stays enabled, describe the product as local case storage plus disclosed, transient third-party processing—not “on-device AI,” “no data leaves your device,” or “zero retention.” Confirm the Cloudflare and Google contracts, subprocessors, regions, transfer safeguards, deletion/retention terms, and whether an enterprise/Vertex AI arrangement is required. Google describes Vertex AI as the option when guaranteed zero retention or enterprise data-processing terms are required; verify the exact model and terms before choosing it.

## App Store and UK regulatory work before release

- **Native product:** create a bundled iOS app with useful native value, rather than only a remote website wrapper. Add native camera/photo selection, secure local case storage, iOS share/export and device authentication where appropriate. Keep the UI available offline for saved cases and test mode; generation may require network access. Review against Apple's minimum-functionality guideline.
- **Privacy policy:** publish a real, accessible privacy policy and link it both in-app and in App Store Connect. It must identify the controller, data collected, device/cloud flows, purposes, recipients, retention, security, rights and deletion/contact process. App Privacy answers must match all enabled code and SDK behavior, including photos sent to AI providers.
- **Permissions:** add accurate camera and photo-library purpose strings in the native target and request access only when the clinician invokes those features. Do not request tracking permission unless cross-company tracking is actually added.
- **Clinical scope:** write the precise intended-purpose statement before deciding whether this is a regulated medical device. It creates AI-generated smile proposals and allows clinician clinical inputs; a disclaimer does not determine device status. Ask MHRA for classification/regulatory advice if the intended use includes influencing treatment planning, bite correction, diagnosis or recommendations. Keep visualisation, not guaranteed outcome, claims evidence-based. If regulated, identify the applicable conformity, quality, clinical evaluation, vigilance and registration path before release.
- **Governance:** identify who is controller (developer, clinic, or each practice), choose and document an Article 6 lawful basis and Article 9 condition, provide a privacy notice to patients, agree processor terms, screen and likely complete a DPIA, set a retention schedule, and prepare incident/data-subject-rights handling. Patients' clinical-record retention obligations may differ from this optional visualisation app's local case library; get the practice's policy before setting defaults.
- **Review package:** complete App Store Connect privacy labels, age rating, support and privacy URLs, app description/screenshots, review notes and a demo path. The demo should not need patient information or paid AI credits. No user accounts currently exist, so account-deletion requirements are not triggered unless accounts are added.

## Decisions and evidence still needed

- Confirmed product direction: local case persistence with Google Gemini processing only after a case-specific clinician confirmation.
- Confirm the app's intended purpose and markets (UK only, or also EU/US); these change regulatory analysis.
- Supply the legal controller/business name, practice contact, privacy-policy URL/contact, retention period and support URL for release materials.
- Have a UK healthcare/privacy lawyer or DPO review the intended purpose, lawful bases, Article 9 condition, patient information/AI permission wording, processor agreements, international transfers and DPIA. This cannot be legally certified from source-code review alone.
